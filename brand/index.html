The Typefaces

Two families, both free to use anywhere. No italics.

Headlines, labels, buttons

Libre Franklin Black

Libre Franklin Bold for the line under a headline, and for every label and button.

A gothic of the kind American newspapers and mailers have used for a century. Set headlines tight and very large.

Reading copy

Gelasio. A plain book serif for everything a person reads at length: paragraphs, the letter, answers to questions. Bold lead-ins open a paragraph. Short paragraphs, generous line spacing, never smaller than 17 pixels on a screen or 10 points in print.

If neither is available: Franklin Gothic or Arial Black for headlines, Georgia for copy.

Always, and Never

Always

  • White or off-white paper. Colour comes in solid bands and boxes.
  • One giant headline per page, in blue, with the key words in red.
  • A red line of capitals above the headline.
  • A real doctor in the first screen or on the cover, named in a caption.
  • Check marks, numbered boxes, tables with visible rules.
  • One red button, with the same words every time: See if I qualify.
  • Yellow highlighter behind one figure or phrase per screen.
  • Short paragraphs with bold lead-ins. Plenty to read.

Never

  • Dark pages, glowing gradients, glass panels.
  • Thin, elegant or futuristic lettering. Italics.
  • Rounded pills, floating cards, soft shadows.
  • Animation that plays by itself.
  • Stock art of cells, DNA or circuits.
  • Income figures, health promises, invented reviews.
  • Names that are not cleared. Other companies' logos or looks.
