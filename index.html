Dear future partner,

Own a stem cell business that is already built

Take a drive through your town this week.

You will pass the signs. Wellness. Longevity. Anti-aging. Stem cells. Two years ago that corner unit sold phones. Today the parking lot is full by nine in the morning, and no insurance company sent a single one of those cars. The people inside are paying out of their own pockets.

One of those offices could be yours.

You do not need a medical license to own one. You own the business, and licensed clinicians handle the medicine.

The market behind those signs is still growing. The Global Wellness Institute puts the wellness economy at $7.5 trillion, up 8.9 percent in a single year.

What stops most people who try

First come the healthcare attorneys, because the law decides who may own what. Then the search for a physician willing to supervise and a clinician trained to do the procedures. Then a supplier you can trust, a way to answer every call, and advertising that keeps everyone out of trouble. A year goes by, and so does a small fortune. Some never open at all.

Here, all of it is handed to you.

Your company is set up for your state by healthcare attorneys. Physician oversight is in place before you open, and your supervising physician is named in writing. A nurse practitioner performs the procedures at your office, with a staffing network that reaches all 50 states behind that hire.

You order from a catalog of more than 20 biologics at partner pricing. Your clinician is trained to perform the procedures, and you and your team are trained to hold the consultations. A pricing playbook shows you how to package what your office offers.

You also receive the system that runs the office. You choose the name, and your website is drafted for you to edit. An assistant answers your phone, your texts and your website day and night and books the consultations. Every inquiry lands in one place, and the follow-up is drafted for you.

Behind all of it stands a clinical network with more than 100 licensed clinicians, more than 6,000 patients treated and more than eight years in regenerative care.

What that gives you

You own the business. The name on the sign is yours, the company is yours, and the accounts are in your company's name.

You open with the hard part finished, in a market where people pay for their own care.

Your office answers its phone at midnight and books the consultation while you sleep. Your patients have a reason to come back, too: twelve minutes in the Longevity Chamber™, offered as a membership.

Your part

You open the office and hire a small team. You run the local advertising, sit down with the people who come in and keep the calendar full. You never diagnose anyone, choose a product or decide who is a candidate. Licensed clinicians do all of it. You are the owner all day, and never once the doctor.

Now picture that drive a year from now.

The road is the same, with one more sign on it, and this one is yours. Your name is on the lease. Your team is at the front desk. A clinician you never had to recruit is with a patient who found you last week.

The price, and the catch

The minimum investment is $100,000. Three numbers decide whether that is worth it to you: what a patient pays, what the physician and the treatment cost you, and how many patients it takes to cover what you put in. Ask us about all three. Financial performance information is provided in the disclosure document, in writing, and you will have that document before you sign or pay anything.

The catch is that this is not open to everyone, and it will not stay open. We are placing a limited number of partnerships. When they are placed, we stop taking applications. Not everyone who asks is accepted.

So do this today.

Ask whether you qualify. It takes about a minute, costs nothing and commits you to nothing. Within two business days you will have the partner brief: whether a partnership is still available, what the $100,000 covers line by line, and what happens between signing and opening. Read it at your kitchen table. Hand it to your attorney. Then decide.

Nothing is signed and nothing is paid until you hold the full disclosure in writing and the waiting period the law requires has passed.
