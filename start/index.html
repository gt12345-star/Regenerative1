Why a doctor wants a partner who is not a doctor

Medical school teaches medicine. It does not teach how to sign a lease, hire a front desk, run an advertisement or fill a calendar. That side is yours. Here is one ordinary Tuesday.

  1. 8:30 am

    Your front desk opens. The day's evaluations are already on the calendar.

  2. 9:00 am

    A new patient sits down for a first evaluation. A licensed clinician takes it from there.

  3. Noon

    You host a lunch-hour education event from an approved script.

  4. 3:00 pm

    Telehealth follow-ups run without you. Clinicians see patients online, and medication ships to their homes.

  5. 5:30 pm

    You lock up. You were the owner all day, and never once the doctor.

Common questions

Do I need a medical license?

No. You own the business: a management and marketing company. Licensed clinicians in a physician-owned practice make every clinical decision and deliver all care.

What does my office offer?

Stem cell and regenerative care, hormones, weight loss programs and a red light chamber. Licensed clinicians deliver or direct all of the care.

Do I need an office?

Yes. Partners operate from a local office, where patients can meet your team in person. If you already run a practice, you have one.

What does the $100,000 cover?

A business that is already built: a compliant structure for your state, physician oversight, a telehealth platform, cleared marketing and a launch team. It is itemized in writing, with every ongoing fee, before you sign.

What can I earn?

It comes down to three numbers: what a patient pays, what the physician and the treatment cost you, and how many patients cover your investment. Any financial performance information is provided in the disclosure document, in writing.

Are the stem cell therapies FDA-approved?

No. They have not been approved by the FDA. A licensed clinician evaluates each patient and decides whether treatment is appropriate.
